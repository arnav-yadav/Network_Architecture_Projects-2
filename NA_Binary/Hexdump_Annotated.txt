WHAT TO NOTICE

1. The header is 8 bytes and always 8 bytes. A reader takes 8, learns
   the length, takes exactly that many more, and is aligned again.
   No scanning, no delimiter, no escaping — which is why a body may
   contain any byte at all, CRLF included.

2. The status is two bytes, not three ASCII characters, and the method
   is one byte, not three. That is most of the binary win: no parsing
   of text into numbers, no case rules, no whitespace ambiguity.

3. Header names cost one byte each when they are in the static table.
   'content-length' is 14 characters in HTTP and one byte here.
   Values stay literal and length-prefixed, so any bytes are legal.

4. Content-Length is still carried, even though every frame already
   has a length. The frame length frames the FRAME; content-length
   describes the whole BODY, which may span several DATA frames.
   Length framing at two levels, exactly as FastCGI does it.

5. END_STREAM is a flag, not a sentinel value in the data. There is
   no byte sequence that could be confused with end-of-message,
   because the marker does not live in the message at all.
